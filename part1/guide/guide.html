What is Medigap?

Original Medicare doesn't pay everything. Medigap (Medicare Supplement) is private insurance that helps pay what Medicare leaves behind: deductibles, coinsurance and copays.

Standardized

Plans are labeled by letter (A–N). A Plan G is the same coverage no matter which carrier sells it.

Price is the difference

Because benefits match, carriers compete on premium, rate-increase history and service.

Works with Original Medicare

You keep your freedom to see any doctor or hospital that accepts Medicare.

Plan G: the gold standard

Plan G is the most popular Medigap policy nationwide because it offers the most comprehensive coverage available to those newly eligible for Medicare.

  • Covers Part A coinsurance & hospital costs
  • Covers Part B coinsurance/copayments
  • Covers the Part A deductible
  • Covers Part B excess charges
  • 80% foreign travel emergency coverage
$283

Your only out-of-pocket medical cost once you have Plan G: the annual Medicare Part B deductible (2026 amount shown).

After that, covered Medicare-approved hospital and outpatient bills are paid at 100%.

Who Plan G fits best: people who see doctors or specialists often, want predictable monthly costs and a "set-it-and-forget-it" plan. The trade-off: higher premiums than less complete plans.

Before you buy

How to compare carrier rates

Since every Plan G offers the same benefits, comparing carriers comes down to these five questions.

  • What is the monthly premium for my age and ZIP code? Rates vary by location and carrier.
  • How is the premium priced? Attained-age, issue-age or community-rated each change over time differently.
  • What is this carrier's rate-increase history? A low starting rate can climb fast.
  • Does the carrier offer household or other discounts?
  • Will I need medical underwriting? Outside your Medigap open enrollment window, many states allow carriers to review your health.
